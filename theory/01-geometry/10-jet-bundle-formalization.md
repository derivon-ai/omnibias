# 01-10 Jet-bundle formalization

## 1. Thesis and status

The whole program has one formal home: an OMBU channel is a **section of a jet
bundle**, collapse is **prolongation**, a finite gap is a **fiber interval**, a
scan is **translation along the base**, an arrangement is a **stratification**,
and an equality locus is a **fiber-product subvariety**. Fixing this vocabulary
is what keeps fifty specs from inventing fifty ontologies.

- **Status**: gated (contact tests; vocabulary, not a discovery; no `omnibias-jetbundle` package)
- **Depends on**: 01-01, 01-02, 01-03, 01-05, 01-09
- **Blocks**: 02-14, 03-11, 04-01, 06-04

This one is a theory chapter rather than a code deliverable, so it will stay at
`concept` even when its vocabulary is in use everywhere.

## 2. Where it lands

**No package.** This is documentation: a chapter in the monograph (spec 06-04)
and a `docs/theory-jets.md` page once the vocabulary is settled. The only code
consequence is naming: modules and types written under other specs should use
these words consistently.

Resisting the urge to ship a `omnibias-jetbundle` package is the correct call
under the "earn independent existence" rule. A formalization with no distinct
runtime is a chapter.

## 3. Prior art in omnibias

- `omnibias.core.bell` — Bell polynomials and Faa di Bruno combinatorics, the
  composition law for jets.
- `omnibias.core.multi_index` — multi-index ordering and the Cauchy-product
  table, the multivariate jet algebra.
- `omnibias.{torch,jax}.jet` — `compose_jet`, `affine_jet`, `layer_jet`,
  `mlp_jet`, `tower_to_jet`, `jet_to_tower`: directional jets through deep
  compositions.
- `omnibias.{torch,jax}.jet_mv` — `mlp_jet_mv`, `layer_jet_mv`,
  `compose_jet_mv`, `identity_jet`, `jet_partials`, `jet_gradient`,
  `jet_hessian`: every mixed partial to total order `N` in one pass.
- `omnibias.variational` — Euler-Poisson functional derivatives, which are jet
  functionals in the classical sense.
- `omnibias.geometry` — metric, connection and curvature, the differential-geometry
  half of the vocabulary.

**Confirmed gap.** The pieces are all there and are used correctly, but nothing
states the unifying structure. The word "jet" in the codebase means "truncated
Taylor coefficient array", which is the *coordinate* description; the geometric
object it coordinatizes is never named.

## 4. Mathematics

### The bundle

Let `E -> X` be a trivial bundle over the input space `X = R^D` with fiber
`R` (a scalar field; vector fields are the obvious extension). The `N`-th jet
bundle `J^N(E)` has fiber coordinates

```
( u, u_i, u_{ij}, ..., u_{i_1 ... i_N} )
```

that is, the value and all partial derivatives to total order `N`. A smooth
section `u : X -> E` prolongs to `j^N u : X -> J^N(E)`.

`omnibias.core.multi_index` is exactly a coordinate chart on the fiber, and
`jet_mv` computes `j^N u` for `u` a network. That is the dictionary entry.

### The five operations in this language

| Program object | Jet-bundle statement |
|---|---|
| collapse of a `K`-pack | evaluation of the `(K-1)` fiber coordinate of `j^N sigma` along `w` |
| multi-pack (01-01) | a **linear functional on a scattered jet**: values of several fiber coordinates at several base points |
| finite gap (`band`, `integral`) | a **fiber interval**: the difference of the order `-1` coordinate (antiderivative) at two base points |
| bias scan (01-02) | the section pulled back along a translation of the base in the `w` direction |
| arrangement (01-03) | a **stratification** of the base by the sign data of several affine functions |
| equality locus (01-09) | the **fiber product** of two sections over a common value, that is a subvariety of the base cut out by a jet condition |
| PDE | a subvariety of `J^N(E)`; a solution is a section whose prolongation lies inside it |
| Lie symmetry (03-11) | a vector field on `E` whose **prolongation** is tangent to that subvariety |

The last two rows are the payoff. Once a PDE is a subvariety of the jet bundle
and `jet_mv` computes prolongations exactly, symmetry search becomes linear
algebra on closed-form quantities (spec 03-11), and residual evaluation becomes
"how far is `j^N u(x)` from the subvariety" in a metric you get to choose.

### Prolongation formula

For a vector field `v = xi^i d/dx^i + phi d/du`, the `N`-th prolongation is

```
pr^N v = v + sum_{|J| <= N} phi^J d/du_J,
phi^J  = D_J ( phi - xi^i u_i ) + xi^i u_{J,i}
```

with `D_J` the total derivative. Every term is a polynomial in jet coordinates,
so `pr^N v` is computable in closed form from `jet_mv` output. This is the
formula spec 03-11 implements; recording it here keeps it in one place.

### Contact structure

`J^N(E)` carries the contact ideal generated by

```
theta^J = du_J - u_{J,i} dx^i
```

A section of `J^N(E)` is *holonomic* (that is, the prolongation of an actual
function) exactly when it annihilates the contact forms. This has a concrete
consequence for the library: **a tower of numbers is not automatically a jet.**
If code assembles derivative estimates from different sources, the result may
violate the contact conditions, and any downstream identity that assumes
holonomy will be subtly wrong. Checking `theta^J ~ 0` is a cheap, meaningful
integration test, and none exists today.

### Why the finite gap is a fiber interval, not a derivative

The `integral` role computes `S(z + b_hi) - S(z + b_lo)` with `S' = sigma`. In
jet language this is the difference of the `-1` coordinate at two base points,
which is *not* a fiber coordinate of `J^N` at a single point: it is a
**nonlocal** functional. That is exactly why the band and integral roles behave
differently from the derivative roles, and why the gauge lift of spec 02-14 is
natural for them: a nonlocal object along a path is what parallel transport is.

Naming this properly prevents a real error, namely treating a band response as
if it were a local jet coordinate.

## 5. Worked example

Take `D = 1`, `N = 2`, and `u(x) = tanh(x)`. Jet coordinates along the section:

```
j^2 u (x) = ( u, u_x, u_xx ) = ( t, 1 - t^2, -2 t (1 - t^2) ),   t = tanh x
```

At `x = 0.5`, `t = 0.4621172`:

```
j^2 u (0.5) = ( 0.4621172, 0.7864477, -0.7268871 )
```

**Contact check.** Move to `x = 0.5 + h` with `h = 1e-4` and verify
`du - u_x dx ~ 0` numerically:

```
u(0.5001) - u(0.5) = 7.864398e-5
u_x(0.5) * h       = 7.864477e-5
residual           = -7.9e-10      ~ O(h^2), consistent with holonomy
```

Now break holonomy deliberately: build a "jet" `(0.4621172, 0.90, -0.7268871)`
by overwriting the first derivative. The same check gives residual `-1.1e-5`,
five orders larger and `O(h)` rather than `O(h^2)`. A contact residual that fails
to decay at the expected rate is a reliable detector of an assembled,
non-holonomic tower.

**The PDE-as-subvariety reading.** The heat equation `u_t = u_xx` is the linear
subvariety `{ u_t - u_xx = 0 }` of `J^2`. A network's prolongation
`j^2 u_theta(x, t)` is a point in `J^2` at each `(x, t)`, and the PDE residual is
its signed distance to that hyperplane in jet coordinates. Everything a PINN
does is: push the section's prolongation toward a subvariety.

## 6. Proposed API

Almost none, deliberately. Two small additions are justified because they turn
the formalism into a test:

```python

    """Rate test: halve h and check the residual drops by ~4x."""
```

Everything else in this spec is naming, and naming belongs in docs and in
docstrings, not in new symbols.

## 7. Practical use cases

1. **A shared vocabulary across fifty specs.** Cheap to provide, expensive to
   skip: without it, "jet", "tower", "band" and "order" drift in meaning between
   packages.
2. **The holonomy test as a real integration test.** Any pipeline that assembles
   derivative estimates from mixed sources (learned, finite-difference,
   closed-form) can be checked for contact consistency.
3. **A precise statement of what a PINN residual is**, which makes the weak-form
   variant (spec 02-04) obviously the same object measured in a different norm.
4. **The formal precondition for symmetry search** (spec 03-11): prolongation is
   defined here, implemented there.
5. **Correct handling of nonlocal roles.** Band and integral are not jet
   coordinates; saying so prevents a class of quiet modelling errors.

## 8. Acceptance gates

This spec is a chapter, so its gates are about correctness of exposition and the
two small tests:

- **G1 contact test works.** `is_holonomic` returns `True` for genuine
  prolongations of analytic functions and `False` for deliberately corrupted
  towers, on at least 200 randomized cases with no misclassification.
- **G2 rate.** The contact residual decays at the second-order rate for
  holonomic jets and first-order for corrupted ones, measured over three
  halvings.
- **G3 vocabulary coverage.** Every term in the dictionary table appears in at
  least one other spec, and no other spec uses a competing term for the same
  object (checked in the consistency pass).

## 10. Honesty and scope

- This is a **reformulation**, not a result. It proves nothing new; it names
  what is already computed. Presenting a change of language as a discovery would
  be exactly the kind of overclaim the program forbids.
- Jet bundles are standard differential geometry. The contribution is the
  dictionary to omnibias objects, not the theory.
- The founding bias collapse (`delta -> 0`) is the operation that produces fiber
  coordinates. Temperature collapse (`beta -> inf`) does **not** live in the jet
  bundle at all: it acts on the base stratification, not on the fiber. Keeping
  those on different sides of the dictionary is itself a useful clarification.
- Nothing here earns a certificate tier.

## 11. Open questions and risks

- **Over-formalization.** Category-theoretic and bundle-theoretic language can
  make simple code look profound without improving it. The test is whether the
  vocabulary prevents a concrete error; the holonomy check does, so that part
  earns its place. Anything that does not should be cut.
- **Vector- and tensor-valued fields** need the appropriate bundle, and the
  gauge case (spec 02-14) needs a principal bundle. The dictionary above is
  written for scalars and must be extended carefully rather than by analogy.
- **Falsifier.** If, after the consistency pass, no other spec is clarified by
  this vocabulary, it is decoration and should be reduced to a glossary.
