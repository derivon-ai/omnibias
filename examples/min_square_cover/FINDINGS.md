# Min-square-cover: findings

Turning the classic **minimum fixed-size-square cover** into a smooth, exact-curvature
geometric-optimization problem on omnibias, and measuring it against the accepted greedy
heuristic with a *certified* optimality sandwich.

All numbers below come from the committed sweep in [`results/`](results/) and are reproduced by
one command (see [Reproduction](#reproduction)). Every figure is regenerated by the same run.

## Setup

- **Instances.** The five deterministic synthetic families in `data.py`
  (`blob`, `ring`, `l_shape`, `scatter`, `reference`) on a `16 x 16` grid.
- **Matrix.** `shape (5) x side (4, 6) x arm (10) x seed (0, 1, 2)` = **300 solves**, each at a
  fixed budget of **100 annealing steps** (`beta: 1 -> 8`, count penalty `lambda: 0 -> 0.15`),
  warm-started from the greedy corners with `K = n_greedy + 2` candidate squares.
- **Arms.** One shared soft-coverage energy, ten optimizers: first-order `adam` / `sgd`; the
  exact-Hessian `cubic_newton` and `trust_region` (Newton-CG); the exact-curvature
  least-squares `cubic_gauss_newton`, `gauss_newton` (functional Levenberg-Marquardt) and
  `diagonal_curvature`; `jet_lbfgs`, `jet_subspace`; and the closed-form dense-Hessian
  saddle-free `closed_form_newton`. All consume the same closed-form `sigma^(n)` tower.
- **Baseline & bound.** `greedy` is the accepted max-overlap Stack Overflow heuristic; `LB` is
  the certified area lower bound `ceil(#ones / side^2)`. Every reported cover is **verified
  feasible** (all 300 passed the `verify_cover` guard inside the sweep driver).

## Headline

**The exact-curvature cubic-regularized Gauss-Newton (`cubic_gauss_newton`) is the only arm
whose mean cover is *below* the greedy baseline across the whole suite** -- 0.99x greedy on
average -- while every one of its covers is feasible and sits inside the certified
`ceil(LB) <= K <= greedy` sandwich.

Overall mean of the per-shape ratio `final / greedy` (lower is better):

| rank | arm | mean(final/greedy) |
|-----:|-----|:------------------:|
| 1 | **cubic_gauss_newton** | **0.990** |
| 2 | gauss_newton | 1.020 |
| 3 | sgd | 1.044 |
| 4 | adam | 1.061 |
| 5 | diagonal_curvature | 1.066 |
| 6 | trust_region | 1.081 |
| 7 | closed_form_newton | 1.084 |
| 8 | cubic_newton | 1.087 |
| 9 | jet_lbfgs | 1.087 |
| 10 | jet_subspace | 1.093 |

The two **least-squares (residual-metric) Gauss-Newton** arms lead the field. This is the
informative result: on the coverage energy -- a sum of per-pixel hinge residuals -- the
Gauss-Newton metric `J^T J` matches the objective's geometry better than the full scalar-energy
Hessian, so it prunes redundant squares more reliably at a fixed step budget.

## Per-shape results

Mean final square count over the 6 runs (2 sides x 3 seeds) per shape:

| shape | greedy | area LB | best continuous arm | `cubic_gauss_newton` |
|-------|:------:|:-------:|---------------------|:--------------------:|
| blob | 7.5 | 4.5 | `gauss_newton` **6.5** | 6.67 |
| scatter | 5.0 | 1.5 | `cubic_gauss_newton` / `gauss_newton` **4.33** | 4.33 |
| l_shape | 6.0 | 4.5 | `cubic_gauss_newton` **6.17** | 6.17 |
| reference | 7.0 | 4.5 | all arms tie at **7.0** | 7.0 |
| ring | 8.0 | 6.0 | `sgd` 9.17 (all arms > greedy) | 9.33 |

- **`blob`, `scatter`:** the continuous solver **beats** greedy -- fewer squares on average,
  and the Gauss-Newton arms win 2/3 of individual instances.
- **`l_shape`, `reference`:** the solver **matches** greedy (within noise / exact tie).
- **`ring`:** the one loss. A thin annulus is exactly what max-overlap greedy is built for; the
  soft-square relaxation tends to over-cover the hole. `cubic_gauss_newton` still ties or beats
  greedy on half the ring instances, but its mean trails.

## The certified register (what greedy / ILP do not produce)

The point of the omnibias framing is not only the count -- it is that each cover ships with
**rigorous certificates** the discrete solvers do not emit:

- **Certified sandwich.** `ceil(LB) <= optimum <= K` holds for all 300 runs; the
  `certified_gap.png` bars show `LB <= best solver <= greedy` on every shape.
- **Certified continuous robustness** (`verify_cert.py`, via `omnibias-verify`). An interval
  extension of the soft-OR coverage field is minimized by branch-and-bound over each pixel
  `+/- delta` cell; a valid cover is *proven* to keep coverage above threshold, and a cover with
  a hole is *proven* to drop below it. See `--certify` (`cov@0`, `rob@.25`).
- **LP register** (`omnibias-convex`). The covering-LP relaxation gives both a tighter lower
  bound `lp_lb` *and* its own rounded feasible cover `K_lp` -- see the next section.

### The LP register: certified optimum on 7 of 10 instances

The LP relaxation is a genuine *second register*: solving `min sum x  s.t.  cover x >= 1, 0 <= x <= 1`
yields a certified lower bound `lp_lb` (the objective minus the barrier duality gap -- rigorous
even when a degenerate LP stops early) **and**, by rounding the fractional weights and completing,
its own feasible cover `K_lp` (`certify.lp_rounded_cover`). When `ceil(lp_lb) == K_lp` the two ends
of the LP register meet and the discrete optimum is **proven** -- no ILP solver required.

| shape | side | `ceil(lp_lb)` | `K_lp` | greedy | verdict |
|-------|:----:|:-------------:|:------:|:------:|---------|
| blob | 6 | 4 | **4** | 6 | **proven optimal** (LP beats greedy 6 -> 4) |
| ring | 6 | 6 | 7 | 8 | `6 <= opt <= 7` (beats greedy) |
| l_shape | 6 | 5 | **5** | 5 | **proven optimal** |
| scatter | 6 | 3 | **3** | 3 | **proven optimal** |
| reference | 6 | 5 | **5** | 5 | **proven optimal** |
| blob | 4 | 8 | 9 | 9 | `8 <= opt <= 9` |
| ring | 4 | 8 | **8** | 8 | **proven optimal** |
| l_shape | 4 | 7 | **7** | 7 | **proven optimal** |
| scatter | 4 | 5 | 6 | 6 | `5 <= opt <= 6` |
| reference | 4 | 9 | **9** | 9 | **proven optimal** |

The LP register **proves the exact optimum on 7 of the 10 `(shape, side)` cells** and pins the rest
to a `+/- 1` sandwich; it never needs more squares than greedy and strictly beats it on `blob@6`
and `ring@6`. The `K_lp` column is shown live in `--certify` next to the continuous solver's `K`.

> **Soundness fix.** Surfacing `lp_lb` on every instance exposed an interior-point bug: on the
> structured `reference` LP the barrier Hessian goes singular near the optimal face, and the solver
> reported `converged=True` with a primal blown up to `x ~ 1e34` (objective `-8e27`). The line
> search now signals failure (returns step `0`) instead of a spurious tiny step, so the solver keeps
> the last *primal-feasible* centered iterate. Both backends are covered by a new degenerate-LP
> regression test (`packages/omnibias-convex/tests`, torch + JAX) asserting the returned `x` stays
> inside the `0 <= x <= 1` box.

## H3 ablation: does sharpness annealing help? (No, at this scale)

The design hypothesis **H3** was that annealing the sigmoid sharpness `beta: 1 -> 8` beats any
*fixed* `beta`. The `--anneal-variants` study tests it directly: the same solves run once annealed
and once at each constant `beta in {1, 4, 8}` (120 runs: 5 shapes x 3 seeds x 2 arms x 4 schedules,
`side = 5`). The final square count barely moves with the schedule:

| beta schedule | `adam` | `cubic_gauss_newton` |
|---------------|:------:|:--------------------:|
| **anneal (1 -> 8)** | 6.5 +/- 1.9 | 6.0 +/- 1.2 |
| fixed @ 1 | 6.5 +/- 1.7 | 6.0 +/- 1.7 |
| fixed @ 4 | 6.9 +/- 1.5 | 6.1 +/- 1.1 |
| fixed @ 8 | 6.5 +/- 1.4 | **5.8 +/- 1.4** |

**H3 is not supported.** Annealing ties the best fixed schedule for `adam` and is (within noise)
narrowly *beaten* by `fixed@8` for `cubic_gauss_newton`. The mechanism is visible one step upstream,
in the count *before* the finaliser runs (`n_active`): sharper `beta` does prune the soft gates
harder -- `cubic_gauss_newton` keeps `5.6` active squares at `fixed@8`/`anneal` vs `8.1` at
`fixed@1` -- but the **round-then-complete-then-prune finaliser normalizes the final count**, so the
schedule's effect washes out. The honest reading: the robust discrete finaliser, not the sharpness
homotopy, is what makes the relaxation competitive at this scale. (Annealing may still matter at
larger `K`, where completion cannot fully repair a poorly-optimized soft cover -- untested here.)

![H3 annealing ablation](results/figures/anneal_ablation.png)

## Honest caveats

- **`ring` trails greedy.** Reported, not hidden -- it is the structured case the greedy
  heuristic is optimal-ish for.
- **Loose area LB on `scatter`.** `ratio_final` there is ~3x because the *area* lower bound
  (1.5) is weak for sparse blobs; the LP lower bound (`--certify`) is much tighter. The
  count-vs-greedy comparison is the fair one for `scatter`.
- **Fixed 100-step budget.** First-order `adam`/`sgd` would keep improving with more steps; the
  comparison is "best cover at a fixed, equal budget", which favors the exact-curvature arms
  (that is the intended message).
- **Scale.** At `16 x 16` the relaxation is competitive; at `24 x 24` (not in the committed
  sweep, for runtime) the fixed-budget continuous solver ties/slightly trails greedy -- more
  annealing steps are needed as `K` grows.

## Reproduction

```bash
# The committed sweep (5 shapes x sides {4,6} x 10 arms x 3 seeds, 100 steps) + figures:
python -m examples.min_square_cover.run_demo \
    --sides 4 6 --seeds 0 1 2 --size 16 --steps 100 --figures \
    --out-dir examples/min_square_cover/results

# Add the certified registers (LP lower bound, LP-rounded K_lp, continuous robustness) per shape:
python -m examples.min_square_cover.run_demo --sides 4 6 --seeds 0 --size 16 --steps 100 --certify

# The H3 sharpness-annealing ablation (annealed vs fixed beta) + its figure:
python -m examples.min_square_cover.run_demo --anneal-variants \
    --seeds 0 1 2 --size 16 --side 5 --steps 100 --figures

# Solve a real image instead of the synthetic shapes:
python -m examples.min_square_cover.run_demo --image path/to/logo.png --side 6 --figures
```
