# From infrastructure to a supported scientific product

The open substrate supplies adoption and reproducible evidence. Dual-licensed
engines offer commercial integration rights. New private products supply complete
industrial workflows, validation, deployment and maintenance. None of these
layers alone guarantees revenue.

## Validate one customer problem

The candidate domains are mesh-free simulation, integrated-circuit optimization,
materials discovery, batteries and neural variational Monte Carlo. Select the
first workflow through access to a paying technical sponsor, usable data, an
expensive measurable problem and budget authority. Avoid parallel product launches
before one workflow has repeatable customer value.

| Period | Action | Decision evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–15 | Interview roughly ten accessible buyers across the candidate domains; check rights and release boundaries | Named sponsor, baseline, usable data, budget and a narrow pilot scope |
| Days 16–45 | Deliver one paid pilot against the customer's accepted baseline | Held-out accuracy, robustness, wall time, peak memory and integration effort |
| Days 46–75 | Seek a second customer with substantially the same problem; package a repeatable deployment | Reusable product work separated from customer-specific integration |
| Days 76–90 | Seek an annual deployment/OEM/service agreement and an approved case study | Repeat use and willingness to pay; otherwise narrow or change the workflow |

A pilot agreement sets thresholds before measurement. Record solver/model size,
precision, hardware, preprocessing, compilation, training and inference separately.
Include failure cases and the customer's baseline. Microbenchmark advantages
motivate investigation; they do not establish end-to-end savings.

## Match the offer to deployment

Internal R&D buys an integrated result and supported operation. OEM buyers need
specified proprietary redistribution rights. Hosted customers buy a managed
service; other vendors may instead need commercial hosting rights. These offers
share engineering but have different contracts and operational commitments.

Customers retain agreed rights to their data and confidential inputs. Contracts
separate existing IP, reusable improvements and custom work. Do not imply that
internal use of AGPL always requires payment or that simulation results become
open source simply because the engine is AGPL.

The [commercial offer](https://github.com/derivon-ai/omnibias/blob/codex/pinn-substrate-split/COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md)
is nonbinding. Binding agreements require IP-counsel review and a named legal
licensor. This roadmap authorizes no customer outreach, product publication or
external commitment.
