# Hilbert XVI: focal/Bautin engine, derived singular return maps, collar membership

This note records four new exact-Q results layered on top of
[HILBERT16-DULAC-CYCLICITY.md](HILBERT16-DULAC-CYCLICITY.md): a genuine
Groebner engine, a Poincare-Lyapunov focal-value / Bautin-ideal engine, a
resonant Poincare-Dulac normal form with a mechanically **derived** (not
declared) Dulac corner map, and a sound collar-membership certificate. None
of it proves physical return-map membership on an arbitrary graphic,
graphic-wide finite cyclicity, or a DRR-case closure.

## 1. The Groebner engine

`omnibias.holonomic._core.groebner` is an exact-`Q` Buchberger
implementation over `PolyN`: `lex` / `grlex` / `degrevlex` monomial orders,
multivariate division with remainder, both classical Buchberger
pair-skipping criteria, a reduced (monic, minimal, interreduced) basis, and
ideal / radical membership returning an exact cofactor witness

\[
f = \sum_i q_i g_i + r,
\]

which `verify_ideal_membership` replays by plain polynomial arithmetic,
without rerunning Buchberger. Radical membership uses Rabinowitsch's trick:
\(f \in \sqrt{(g_1,\ldots,g_k)}\) iff \(1 \in (g_1,\ldots,g_k,\,1-t f)\) in
the ring extended by one fresh variable \(t\). Buchberger's algorithm is
doubly exponential in the worst case, so every entry point takes a
`GroebnerBudget` and raises `GroebnerBudgetExceeded` loudly rather than
running unbounded.

## 2. Poincare-Lyapunov focal values

Given a monodromic linear part normalized (`normalize_monodromic`, exact
whenever the determinant is a rational square) to

\[
x'=-y+P(x,y),\qquad y'=x+Q(x,y),
\]

the classical successive-approximation construction builds a formal first
integral \(F=(x^2+y^2)/2+F_3+F_4+\cdots\) degree by degree. The rotation
operator \(L(f)=-y\,\partial_x f+x\,\partial_y f\) is bijective on
homogeneous polynomials of odd degree and has a one-dimensional cokernel on
homogeneous degree \(2m\) spanned by \((x^2+y^2)^m\); the obstruction there
is the focal (Poincare-Lyapunov) value \(V_m\).
`omnibias.dynamics.focal.lyapunov_quantities` solves this homological
equation exactly over \(\mathbb Q\) (or \(\mathbb Q[\text{params}]\)),
using plain exact rational linear algebra and the integer null-space solver
already shipped in `omnibias.core.proof.lift`.

The one-dimensional kernel of \(L\) at each even degree is a genuine gauge
freedom, fixed here by requiring the new term to be orthogonal to a
declared left-null covector -- a specific, reproducible choice, not *the*
literature convention. **The first nonvanishing \(V_m\) in a run is a
genuine gauge-independent dynamical invariant** (a weak focus of that
order, by the classical Poincare-Lyapunov theorem); values computed after
it are gauge-dependent, which the regression suite checks directly by
re-normalizing the same system with a different gauge and confirming the
values up to and including the first nonzero one agree while later ones
differ. `certify_focal_values` seals the single `F_dot` homological
identity as a `polynomial_identity_q` obligation.

## 3. The Bautin ideal

`omnibias.dynamics.bautin.bautin_basis` is the reduced Groebner basis of
the ideal \((V_1,\ldots,V_N)\) generated by every computed focal value.
That **ideal** is gauge-invariant (unlike any single \(V_k\) beyond the
first nonzero one) and its zero set is exactly the truncated center
variety. `cyclicity_bound = basis_length - 1` is reported alongside
`bautin_ideal_stabilization_proved: False` on every basis this module
produces: confirming that every later generator stays in the current ideal
is exactly the missing all-orders content, and this module gives the
machinery to *check* stabilization through a finite order, not a proof that
it holds in general. `finite_order_stabilization_verified` records that
narrow result separately. The jet-only obstruction is tested in
[HILBERT16-BAUTIN-STABILIZATION-BARRIER.md](HILBERT16-BAUTIN-STABILIZATION-BARRIER.md).

Bautin's normalized quadratic weak-focus family (`bautin_quadratic_family`,
5 free parameters) is a literature-replay target: the regression suite
cross-checks the resulting basis length of three against an independent
`sympy` Groebner computation of the same three focal-value polynomials --
never against a hand-recalled literature formula.

## 4. Resonant normal form and a derived corner map

`omnibias.dynamics.saddle_normal_form.resonant_normal_form` diagonalizes a
checked hyperbolic saddle with **exact rational** eigenvalues
\(\lambda_1<0<\lambda_2\) (`diagonalize_saddle`) and removes every
*non-resonant* monomial degree by degree: at homogeneous degree \(k\), a
monomial \(\xi^i\eta^j\) in the \(\xi\)-equation has eigenvalue
\(i\lambda_1+j\lambda_2-\lambda_1\) under the linearized flow generator
(symmetrically \(-\lambda_2\) in the \(\eta\)-equation); a nonzero
eigenvalue clears the monomial by one exact division, and a zero eigenvalue
marks a **resonant** monomial that cannot be removed by any polynomial
change of variables -- exactly where a Dulac \(x^r\log x\) term originates.

`dulac_corner_expansion` derives the corner map's leading correction via
the classical conserved-quantity perturbation argument: let
\(H(\xi,\eta)=\xi^{\lambda_2}\eta^{\mu_1}\) (\(\mu_1=-\lambda_1\)), the
exact first integral of the *linear* part. Tracking one orbit from
\((\xi,\eta)=(1,x)\) to \((\xi,\eta)=(y,1)\), the linear part alone gives
the classical leading order \(y=x^r\) (\(r=\mu_1/\lambda_2\)). The
resonant nonlinear terms perturb \(\log H\) by
\(d(\log H)/dt=\lambda_2 p^\star(\xi,\eta)/\xi+\mu_1 q^\star(\xi,\eta)/\eta\);
substituting the leading-order orbit relation \(\xi=(x/\eta)^r\) inside that
already-small correction and integrating from \(\eta=x\) to \(\eta=1\)
gives

\[
\log y = r\log x - K(x)\log x,
\]

i.e. \(y = x^r - K(x)\log(x)\,x^r + (\text{uncomputed higher-order
terms})\), where every resonant monomial's contribution to \(K\) lands at a
*single* \(x\)-exponent -- a direct algebraic consequence of the resonance
condition. **This module deliberately stops at first order and does not
exponentiate** \(\exp(-K(x)\log x)\): the hand-solvable one-resonant-term
Bernoulli case (\(\lambda_1=-1\), \(\lambda_2=1\),
\(p^\star=c\,\xi^2\eta\), exact closed form
\(y=x/(1+cx\log x)\)) shows the \(\exp\) resummation reproduces the linear
term in \(cx\log x\) correctly but gets the *next* term's coefficient wrong
(\(1/2\) instead of the true \(1\)) -- so exponentiating would silently
claim a second-order accuracy this derivation does not have. Truncating at
plain first order is exactly what it claims to be, and is cross-checked
against that exact solvable case (and against direct numerical integration
of the source ODE) in the regression suite.

`certify_resonant_normal_form` seals a truncated conjugacy identity (one
`polynomial_identity_q` payload) that `verify_resonant_normal_form` replays
by plain polynomial arithmetic, not by re-running the recursion.
`derive_return_map` then composes the corner expansion with a certified
regular-arc return Jacobian from
`omnibias.dynamics.return_maps.certify_stopped_event`, landing on
`RationalInterval` coefficients via a new `DerivedDulacExpansion` wrapper
kept off `DulacExpansion` itself, so every existing sealed digest stays
byte-identical.

## 5. Collar membership

`omnibias.dynamics.membership.certify_collar_membership` proves the
declared finite Dulac model (`GraphicTarget.return_map`) and the
field-derived corner expansion soundly **agree** -- as interval
enclosures, never a single float -- on a collar \(x\in[\delta,\delta_0]\)
bounded away from the corner, reusing the same `enclose_dulac_term`
interval machinery already sealed for the Dulac-model certificates. It
splits the collar into exact-rational sub-boxes; `status` is
`PROVED_COLLAR` only when *every* sub-box's two enclosures overlap. When
the collar is proved, an optional `interval_newton` pass on the declared
displacement additionally certifies a genuine unique limit cycle inside
the collar when the enclosure allows it -- a real positive result, but
still local to the collar, not a graphic-wide finite-cyclicity theorem.
`corner_window_external` stays `True` on every certificate, and the
narrow `collar_return_membership_proved` flag is kept separate from
`physical_return_membership_proved`, which stays `False`.

## 6. Formal and honesty boundary

Every certificate in this note carries, at minimum:

```text
physical_return_membership_proved = false
graphic_finite_cyclicity_proved = false
drr_case_closed = false
full_hilbert16_solved = false
```

The focal-value certificate additionally carries
`bautin_ideal_stabilization_proved = false`; the resonant normal-form
certificate carries `resonant_monomials_derived_not_declared = true`; the
collar-membership certificate carries `corner_window_external = true`.

The seven benchmark gates (`benchmarks/hilbert16_focal.py`) are:

- GF1: the Groebner engine (reduced basis, cofactor-witness replay, hard
  budget refusal);
- GF2: focal values (a nonzero-\(V_1\) numeric focus vs. a Hamiltonian
  structural zero, round-trip, tamper rejection);
- GF3: the Bautin ideal (basis length three on the classical quadratic
  family, center membership);
- GF4: the resonant normal form (zero resonances on the named rational
  saddle, round-trip, tamper rejection);
- GF5: the derived corner expansion (the hand-solvable Bernoulli case,
  regular-arc composition scaling);
- GF6: collar membership (a blocked disagreement, a proved agreement, and a
  genuine unique-cycle proof);
- GF7: wiring every certificate into `GraphicTarget` /
  `certify_graphic_cyclicity` and replaying the resulting honesty flags.
